# aero-dash-selection

A beginner-friendly aeromodelling assignment covering airfoil selection, paper plane design, RC aircraft development methodologies, and component selection through practical engineering challenges.

## Problem Statements

### Problem 1 — Airfoil Selection and Aerodynamic Analysis (25 marks, +5 bonus)
Design two RC aircraft: **A**, optimized for maximum payload while maintaining stable flight; **B**, optimized for maneuverability (turns, rolls, loops). Identify a suitable airfoil for each. Research and shortlist at least three candidate airfoils per aircraft, select one with justification, compare lift/drag coefficients, L/D ratio, stall, camber, and thickness, and discuss whether one airfoil can serve both missions. Distinguish airfoil traits from whole-aircraft factors. Bonus compares shortlisted airfoils in XFLR5/XFOIL with plots.

### Problem 2 — The Paper Plane Challenge (25 marks, +5 bonus)
Build a paper airplane (single A4 sheet, no tape/glue/clips/propulsion, manual launch) that stays airborne longest. Document construction, explain wing shape/dimensions, analyze lift/weight/drag/thrust, wing loading, CG, and stability. Complete at least five flight trials, record durations, compute the mean, and propose two modifications with test plans. Bonus builds a second design and compares averages.

### Problem 3 — Simulation vs Practical Development (25 marks, +5 bonus)
As lead designer, choose and defend a development approach for an RC aircraft given limited time/money/resources. Compare simulation-led vs prototype-led design across cost, time, accuracy, iteration speed, failure detection, and performance. Propose a full workflow, name three parameters to simulate and three to verify physically, explain validation, and diagnose a simulated plane that cannot take off. Bonus plans a four-week schedule with contingency.

### Problem 4 — Bill of Materials for an RC Aircraft (25 marks, +5 bonus)
Prepare a complete BOM for a functional electric single-motor fixed-wing RC aircraft (1–1.2 m wingspan, conventional/high wing, wireless control, 1–1.2 m wingspan). Identify all components, compare at least two alternatives per major component with specs/quantity/INR price/source links, justify selections, and estimate total cost with budget- and performance-oriented configurations. Bonus estimates takeoff mass and propulsion sufficiency.

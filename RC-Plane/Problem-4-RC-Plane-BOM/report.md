Purpose: Research-based design proposal; no components purchased or flight tests performed.

1. Objective and Design Assumptions
The objective is to prepare a complete, cost-conscious bill of materials (BOM) for a functional RC aircraft with a wingspan between 1 m and 1.2 m. The aircraft should be stable for a beginner, easy to repair, and suitable for iterative development.

The proposed aircraft uses a high-wing layout because it provides a practical arrangement for a trainer-style aircraft. A foam-board airframe reinforced with a lightweight spar is selected for affordability and repairability.

Design Assumptions
Wingspan: 1.1 m.

Wing planform: Approximately rectangular, with a target wing area of 0.22–0.26 m².

Propulsion: One brushless outrunner motor and one propeller.

Battery: 3-cell lithium-polymer (3S LiPo), nominal voltage 11.1 V.

Control surfaces: Elevator, rudder, and ailerons.

Radio: At least four independently usable control channels; a six-channel system is preferred.

Landing: Small landing gear if the final design supports safe ground takeoff; otherwise, a hand-launch configuration can be considered.

Prices: Indicative Indian retail prices in INR, excluding any unlisted shipping, taxes, or tools.

The final mass, thrust, and flight time must be confirmed using the completed airframe and manufacturer data. They are not measured results.

5. Motor–Propeller Matching and Thrust Assessment
A brushless motor's KV rating describes its approximate unloaded rotational speed per volt. A 1000 KV motor supplied at a nominal 11.1 V has a theoretical unloaded speed of approximately:

1000 × 11.1 = 11,100 RPM

Actual RPM under load will be lower. Propeller diameter and pitch influence current draw, thrust, and motor temperature. A propeller that is too large or has too much pitch can overload the motor or ESC.

The A2212 1000 KV listings describe operation with 2S–3S batteries and propellers around 10 inches, but these are not sufficient by themselves to prove that a particular propeller is safe on the proposed aircraft. The final selection should be based on the exact motor manufacturer's propeller/current data or a controlled static thrust test using a wattmeter and thrust stand.

Verification Procedure
Confirm the motor's permitted voltage, current, and propeller range.

Select a propeller from the motor manufacturer's recommendations.

Secure the motor test rig and keep all people clear of the propeller plane.

Measure current and voltage with a suitable wattmeter.

Measure static thrust using a suitable thrust stand.

Reject the combination if current exceeds the motor, ESC, battery, or connector limits.

No static thrust measurement has been performed for this proposal.

6. ESC Rating and Electrical Margin
An ESC controls motor speed and must tolerate the maximum expected motor current. A 30 A ESC is a reasonable candidate for this class of aircraft only if the final motor–propeller–battery combination stays within its ratings.

A practical selection rule is:

I_ESC, continuous ≥ 1.25 × I_expected maximum

For example, if a verified motor–propeller combination draws 16 A at full throttle:

1.25 × 16 = 20 A

An ESC rated for at least 20 A continuous would satisfy this simplified margin rule; a suitable 30 A unit would provide additional headroom. This example is a design calculation, not a measured current for the proposed aircraft.

The ESC's battery-cell range and BEC output must also be checked. The BEC powers the receiver and servos, so its voltage and current capability must be sufficient for all installed servos. Never assume that the BEC can safely power any number of servos simply because the ESC current rating is high.

7. Battery Selection and Flight-Time Trade-offs
A 3S LiPo has a nominal voltage of 11.1 V and a fully charged voltage of 12.6 V. A 2200 mAh battery has a nominal capacity of 2.2 Ah.

A battery's theoretical current capability based on its C rating is:

I_rated = C × capacity in Ah

For a 2200 mAh battery rated at 40C:

40 × 2.2 = 88 A

This is a rating-based calculation, not a guarantee that the battery can deliver that current continuously under all conditions. The manufacturer's limits, connector rating, temperature, and battery condition still matter.

A higher-capacity battery can increase potential endurance, but it also increases mass. The correct battery should be chosen by balancing current capability, physical fit, centre of gravity, and aircraft mass.

LiPo Safety Requirements: Use a compatible balance charger, charge on a suitable non-combustible surface under supervision, prevent short circuits, avoid punctures and swelling, and never leave a charging battery unattended. Damaged or swollen batteries must not be flown.

8. Servos and Control Surfaces
The proposed design uses four servos:

Aileron servo — left: Controls the left aileron.

Aileron servo — right: Controls the right aileron.

Elevator servo: Controls pitch.

Rudder servo: Controls yaw.

The ESC supplies throttle control through the receiver and does not require a separate throttle servo.

Two aileron servos allow independent adjustment and can be connected to separate receiver channels or a suitable mix, depending on the radio system. Before flight, verify that every control surface moves in the correct direction and that linkages cannot bind.

For a small foam-board trainer, 9 g servos are a common low-cost option. Metal-geared servos can provide greater durability, but the actual torque, dimensions, operating voltage, and mass should be checked against the control-surface loads.

10. Compatibility Checks
Before assembly and any powered test, confirm all of the following:

The motor is rated for the chosen battery voltage (3S LiPo, 11.1 V).

The propeller is explicitly compatible with the motor and battery combination.

The ESC supports the battery cell count (3S) and expected current.

The battery connector matches the ESC lead, with correct polarity.

The battery's discharge capability (40C) is suitable for the expected current.

The BEC provides the required voltage and sufficient current for the receiver and servos.

The transmitter and receiver use the same compatible radio protocol (AFHDS 2A) and can bind successfully.

The receiver has enough channels for throttle, elevator, rudder, and ailerons.

Servos operate at the receiver/BEC voltage and have adequate torque.

The propeller clears the fuselage, landing gear, and ground.

Motor mounting, fasteners, control horns, hinges, and pushrods are secure.

The centre of gravity is within the aircraft designer's specified range.

All controls move in the correct direction.

A radio-range and failsafe check is completed according to the equipment instructions.

The aircraft should not be flown until structural integrity, radio control, propulsion, balance, and local operating requirements have been checked.

Thrust-to-Weight Calculation
The weight force corresponding to an estimated mass of 826 g is:

W = mg = 0.826 kg × 9.81 m/s² ≈ 8.10 N

For a hypothetical measured static thrust of 700 g-force (a figure cited in the Robu.in product description for a similar A2212 setup), the thrust-to-weight ratio is:

Thrust-to-Weight Ratio = 700 / 826 ≈ 0.85

This hypothetical ratio would be below 1:1, meaning static thrust is lower than aircraft weight. It does not automatically prove that the aircraft cannot fly, because a fixed-wing aircraft generates lift as it moves through the air. However, it may indicate limited acceleration and climb performance, and it is not a substitute for a validated takeoff assessment.

For a trainer, the objective is to establish sufficient thrust and acceptable power loading without overloading the motor, ESC, or battery. The actual thrust must be measured or obtained from reliable data for the exact motor, propeller, and battery combination. The estimated mass and hypothetical thrust above are not evidence that this proposed aircraft has achieved adequate propulsion.

12. Safe Cost-Saving Opportunities
Use locally available foam board and replace only damaged structural pieces.

Keep the initial airframe design simple and avoid unnecessary cosmetic components.

Use a motor/ESC combo only after checking that the included ESC and propeller suit fixed-wing use.

Choose a battery that meets the verified electrical requirements rather than buying the highest C rating available.

Use a compatible six-channel radio system that can be reused for later aircraft.

Standardize connectors and fasteners to simplify maintenance.

Avoid saving money by using an undersized ESC, poor-quality battery, unsuitable propeller, or unreliable radio equipment.

13. Final Recommendation
The recommended starting point is a 1.1 m high-wing foam-board trainer using a reinforced wing, a compatible brushless motor and ESC, a 3S LiPo battery, four servos, and a six-channel radio system.

The budget-oriented configuration is the preferred concept because it balances affordability, repairability, and adequate control-system flexibility. Its estimated total is ₹11,188, but several line items require exact product verification before the total can be treated as a purchasing quote.

The bonus mass estimate is approximately 826 g. Propulsion sufficiency remains unverified until the final motor–propeller–battery combination is checked against reliable specifications and, ideally, tested on a suitable thrust stand. No physical build, flight, or thrust test is claimed in this report.
